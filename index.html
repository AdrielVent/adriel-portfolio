Featured Engineering Project

CPU Heat Sink Thermal Design: 15-Fin Al-6061 Forced-Convection Heat Sink

ENGG 114 Heat Transfer Design Project · Analytical Heat Transfer + MATLAB + Fusion 360 FEA + DFMA

Public Engineering Project Simulation-Supported Prototype Validation Required

This project developed a forced-convection heat sink for a desktop CPU chip with a 100 W steady-state heat load, 25°C ambient air, an official chip temperature limit of ≤85°C, and an internal derated target of ≤70°C. The final selected design is a 15-fin Al-6061 plate-fin heat sink derived from a 17-fin thermal baseline.

MATLAB analytical modeling predicted a total thermal resistance of 0.4485 °C/W and a chip temperature of 69.85°C. Fusion 360 steady-state thermal FEA predicted a maximum temperature of 69.775°C under identical boundary conditions (<0.15% variance). The 15-fin design was selected over the 17-fin thermal optimum to maintain a 2.557 mm clear spacing, preventing hydrodynamic boundary layer merging while achieving a 27.5% mass reduction (54.6 g vs. 75.3 g).

Design Decision: Thermal vs. DFMA

The 17-fin baseline achieved a lower theoretical temperature (61.5°C), but the selected 15-fin design is the superior engineering choice. It maintains adequate airflow channel width (>2.5 mm) to prevent boundary layer interference while reducing mass by 27.5% and ensuring tool clearance during milling.

Boundary Conditions & Assumptions

Assumed uniform volumetric base heat generation, laminar forced convection with ambient air at V = 1.8 m/s, constant thermophysical fluid properties evaluated at film temperature, and adiabatic fin tip conditions.

Technical Honesty & Verification

MATLAB and Fusion 360 results are simulation-supported predictions. Physical validation requires mounting thermocouples to a copper heater block at 100 W dissipation in an environmental chamber before flight-critical deployment.
