Featured project · Solo · Spring 2026

A 15-fin heat sink for a 100 W CPU

ENGG 114 Heat Transfer, Hofstra. Design, calculations, MATLAB model, CAD, and Fusion 360 study are my own work.

Analysis + simulation Not tested on hardware

The problem

Keep a 50.8 × 50.8 mm CPU at or below 85°C while it dissipates 100 W in 25°C air, using the existing mounting hardware. I also set a stricter 70°C target for myself to leave margin.

What I compared

Three concepts: a copper pin-fin heat sink with natural convection, an aluminum folded-fin heat-pipe design, and an Al-6061 plate-fin heat sink with a fan. I picked the plate-fin, then ran a fin-count trade study in MATLAB.

  1. Why 15 fins, not 17

    The 17-fin version ran cooler in Fusion 360 (61.5°C vs 69.8°C). I chose 15 anyway: the wider 2.557 mm gap is easier to machine and leaves room for tools, and the mass drops 27.5% (75.3 g to 54.6 g).

  2. What the numbers mean

    69.85°C is the MATLAB chip temperature with zero contact resistance. 69.775°C is the hottest point in the Fusion 360 model. They are different quantities, so I don't treat them as a match. The convection coefficient (65 W/m²K) is assumed, not measured.

  3. What's still uncertain

    The margin to my 70°C target is only 0.15°C. Adding just 0.0031°C/W of contact resistance puts the chip at 70.16°C, which is over my target but still well under the 85°C requirement. Next step is the 100 W bench test in the report's test plan.
